Relax — this was a safe simulation.

This page did not steal passwords, tokens, corporate data, or cookies from other websites. It created one harmless demo cookie for this website only, then showed that a page can read its own non-HttpOnly cookies.

The real risk is that an unknown QR code can send people to a phishing page, fake SSO page, malicious download, or tracking page. That is where IAM and security awareness become important.

🍪 Cookie truth JavaScript can read only cookies available to the current website and path. It cannot read cookies from unrelated websites.
🚫 HttpOnly protection Cookies marked HttpOnly are not readable by JavaScript. This is why sensitive session cookies should use HttpOnly where possible.
🎣 Phishing risk A QR code can lead to a fake login screen. The page may look real, but the credentials go to the attacker.
🔐 IAM lesson MFA, least privilege, conditional access, session expiry, and access reviews reduce the blast radius if a user is tricked.

QR safety checklist

1. Pause before scanning Ask: who placed this QR code, why is it here, and does it look tampered with?
2. Check the destination After scanning, inspect the URL before entering credentials or downloading anything.
3. Be suspicious of login pages Fake SSO pages are common. Open trusted apps directly instead of through random QR links.
4. Report suspicious QR codes If the QR code appears in an unusual place, report it to the security team.
5. Protect identity Use MFA, avoid password reuse, and never approve unexpected MFA prompts.
6. Think IAM Access should be granted only to the right identity, from the right device, at the right time, for the right reason.

Myth vs fact

Myth: Scanning a QR code instantly gives away all my cookies. Fact: A normal website cannot read cookies from other websites. But it can send you to phishing, set/read its own cookies, or trick you into sharing data.
Myth: If a website knows my browser and timezone, I am hacked. Fact: Many websites can see basic browser signals. The risk comes when those signals are combined with phishing, tracking, or credential capture.
Myth: MFA means I do not need to worry about QR phishing. Fact: MFA helps a lot, but attackers may still try fake MFA prompts, session theft, or social engineering.
Myth: IAM is only an IT problem. Fact: IAM works best when users also make good decisions: verify links, report suspicious activity, and use access responsibly.
